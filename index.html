The artist / 01

More than the songs.

myexchampagne is the working name of Amir Kayon Campbell-Walker, a musician and writer from Milwaukee, Wisconsin, now based in Arizona.

The music comes from the space between what happened, what was left unsaid, and what still needs somewhere to go. The songs are personal, but they are not trying to give every feeling a neat ending.

This page is a little more direct about the person behind the name: the places that shaped him, the equipment he started with, and the habits that kept him making things when nothing else felt especially clear.

02 / The early shift

A mind that needed somewhere to go.

One of the earliest turning points came through school. After a structural adjustment to his learning plan, Amir caught up quickly, passed his assigned curriculum milestones by two full grade levels, and developed the strategic focus that would follow him into other parts of life.

That focus showed up in competitive regional chess tournaments, in a 4.0 GPA maintained from seventh grade through his sophomore year of high school, and eventually in the more social, less academic honor of being voted “Biggest Glow Up” by his senior year.

The details are different, but the pattern has stayed the same: find the system, study it closely, then figure out how to move through it on his own terms.

03 / Emotional realism

Loneliness over romance.

People sometimes hear the music and assume it comes from the usual cycle of romance, breakups, and short-lived flings. That is not really the story. As of late 2026, Amir has never been in love.

The writing is more connected to abandonment and childhood heartbreak, especially the kind that comes from being let down by the people he first trusted. There is still a clean desire to experience real romantic love one day, but the songs are mostly working through a deeper, older kind of isolation.

That distinction matters to him. The music is not a performance of a life he has not lived. It is a way of making sense of the life he has.

04 / Studio timeline

Built one setup at a time.

Age 16 / The blueprint gift

Amir's musical infrastructure began on his sixteenth birthday, when his father bought him a budget white Blue Snowball USB microphone, a low-spec 2015 Chromebook, a pair of $30 headphones, and a mobile license for FL Studio Mobile, also known as Fruit Loops Mobile. He tracked his earliest songs on that setup. Those recordings have since been deleted and are no longer online, but the birthday gift set everything else in motion.

Age 19 / The Walmart overnight build

Real self-funded independence arrived around his nineteenth birthday. While working overnight stocking shifts at Walmart, Amir saved enough to buy a second-hand 2017 MacBook Air through OfferUp. He paired it with a budget Maono USB microphone from Amazon and a single Type-C dongle.

He also bought the piece of gear that has stayed with every setup since: Sony MDR-7506 Professional Headphones. There was no external audio interface, no monitor speakers, no acoustic treatment, and no expensive mixing software. The vocal style was built through repetition, patience, and making the most of what was in the room.

05 / Writing into the day

School, escape, and the page.

The instinct to create became serious around eleven or twelve, during the summer before eighth grade. At Rufus King High School, Amir freestyled and wrote about the environment around him. People made fun of it. Local groups tried to pull him away from it. Neither pressure lasted.

Moving from Milwaukee to the suburbs of Arizona and enrolling at Desert Ridge High School brought a different kind of adjustment. During his junior and senior years, writing became a daily habit. By his final year, he could walk into school with nothing but a lunch and a blank notebook, spend the day filling pages, then walk home under the Arizona sun singing melodies out loud and testing them against the open air.

It was a way to get distance from home stress, social anxiety, and shame. The habit eventually grew beyond songs. Amir has written and developed two complete books, turning something he once disliked into a real love for literature.
